# Rules

These are the rules of the New York Stamp Club, in eight articles, adopted in 1951 and amended from time to time, usually after an incident.

> [!note] Last amended March 10, 2026
> Article VII was amended at the annual meeting of 2026 to say where the loupe goes. The [minutes](/tagged/minutes) record the debate, and the vote.

## Name

The club is called the New York Stamp Club. It was founded in 1951 in the back room of Mr. Alvin Kessler's stationer's shop on Fulton Street, and it is not affiliated with any other society, dealer or stationer.

## Purpose

The club exists to look at stamps, to talk about them, and to help anyone who is curious to do the same. It does not exist to buy or sell them, except at the spring auction, and then only among friends.

## Membership

1. Anyone may join who pays the year's dues and signs the book. Membership runs for the calendar year; a member who joins after September is paid up to the end of the following year.
2. Dues are twelve dollars a year, payable in cash or in mint commemoratives of equal face value.
3. Members under eighteen pay no dues.
4. Guests may attend any meeting, without limit, and are asked only to sign the visitors' book.

## Officers

The club has four officers: a president, a treasurer, a secretary and a librarian. They are elected at the annual meeting for two years, and may stand again. The secretary keeps the minutes and edits the newsletter; the librarian keeps the reading list and the club's maps.

## Meetings

The club meets on the second Tuesday of each month. The annual meeting is held before the talk at the March meeting, and fifteen members make a quorum.[^1] A special meeting may be called by any ten members writing to the secretary.

## Money

The treasurer keeps the dues and the kettle fund in separate accounts, and reports on both at the annual meeting. No officer may spend more than fifty dollars without asking the committee, except on tea.

## Conduct

1. Stamps are handled with tongs.
2. The price of a stamp is not discussed until after the tea.
3. The loupe goes round the room clockwise, and it must come back to the librarian.
4. A member who answers a visitor's question will answer all of it.

## Amendment

These rules may be amended at an annual meeting by two-thirds of the members present, provided the amendment was sent to the secretary a month before. Amendments to Article VII may also be proposed from the floor, because they always are.

[^1]: The quorum was nine until 1988, when the club had grown and the annual meeting had not. It was raised to fifteen on a motion from Inez Calloway, who said she was tired of counting the same people twice.
