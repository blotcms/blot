# Join

Membership is twelve dollars a year, payable in cash or, by a tradition older than anyone present, in mint commemoratives of equal face value. A dozen one-dollar stamps will do; so will two sixes. The treasurer is fair about change, and has opinions about which commemoratives.

{||} **Members** pay $12 a year. They get a seat at every meeting and a vote at the annual one in March; the use of the loupe, the tongs and the good lamp; *The Perforation*, our newsletter, twice a year on paper; a place at the outing, the Christmas party and the annual dinner; and a say in what we read.

{||} **Guests** pay nothing, though the hat goes round. They get a seat at any meeting, as often as they like; tea, and the loupe when it comes round; the summer and winter lectures, which are open to everyone; and no vote, which means no one will ask them to serve on anything.

## How to sign up

Come to any meeting and speak to the treasurer, Priya Venkataraman, at the table by the door. She will take your twelve dollars, or its equivalent in stamps, and write your name in the book.

If you can't come in person, write to join@nystampclub.example with your name, your address and a sentence about what you collect, even if it is nothing yet. We will write back with the details and a place to send your dues.

> [!tip] Come twice first
> Most members came as guests two or three times before they joined, and we recommend it. You will know by the second evening whether the arguments are your kind of arguments.

Membership is governed by Article III of the club's [rules](/rules), which is short.

## Questions

<details><summary>When does my membership run from?</summary>From the meeting at which you join, for one year. Priya will remind you, in person, a month before it ends.</details>
<details><summary>Do children pay?</summary>No. Members under eighteen join free, and the [kettle fund](/support) pays their dues. Marguerite Fong runs the juniors' table and will find them something to start with.</details>
<details><summary>I stopped collecting in 1974. Can I still join?</summary>Yes. Several members did the same, and they are the ones who know what everything used to cost.</details>
