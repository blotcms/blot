# About

The New York Stamp Club has met on the second Tuesday of every month since 1951, first in the back room of a stationer's on Fulton Street and now in the members' room of the Society Library.
## How it began

In the spring of 1951 a stationer on Fulton Street, Mr. Alvin Kessler, noticed that the same eleven people kept coming in on the second Tuesday to ask whether the new commemoratives had arrived. He gave them the back room, a table and a kettle, and asked only that they buy their mounts from him. They did, for nineteen years, and the club's first minutes record that the kettle was the better investment.

When the stationer's closed in 1970 we moved four times in six years, and in 1976 the Society Library gave us the members' room. We have been there since, and we mind the radiators.

## What we do

Each meeting has a talk, a table of things to look at, and tea. Talks are given by members and run to about forty minutes; the rest of the evening is for arguing. Once a year we go on an outing, and in the winter we read aloud, usually from *Household Words*, the magazine Charles Dickens edited in the 1850s, which was fascinated by the post. You can read what we read in [Readings](/tagged/readings).

## Our quirks

- We do not talk about the price of a stamp until after the tea.
- Tongs are provided. Fingers are discouraged.
- The treasurer may be paid in mint commemoratives of equal face value. This is the tradition, and she has opinions about it.
- Visitors may ask any question, but the member who answers it will answer all of it.
- The loupe goes round the room, and it must come back.

The club is run by a small committee of volunteers; you can meet them on the [Members](/members) page. If you would like to come along, [visit](/visit) on any second Tuesday, or [join](/join).
