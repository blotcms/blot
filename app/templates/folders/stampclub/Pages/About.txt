# About

::: lead
The New York Stamp Club has met on the second Tuesday of every month since 1951: first in the back room of a stationer's on Fulton Street, and since 1976 in the members' room of the Society Library on East Seventy-ninth Street.
:::

::: figures
- **1951** the first meeting, in a stationer's back room
- **75** years of second Tuesdays
- **900** meetings, give or take a blizzard
- **150** issues of *The Perforation*
:::

## How it began

{|<} The founders' minute book survives, in pencil, and records the price of everything the club bought in its first year, down to the mounts.

In the spring of 1951 Mr. Alvin Kessler, who kept a stationer's on Fulton Street, noticed that the same eleven people came in on the second Tuesday of every month to ask whether the new commemoratives had arrived. He gave them his back room, a table and a kettle, and asked only that they buy their mounts from him. They did, for nineteen years.

When the shop closed in 1970 the club moved four times in six years: to a church hall, a dentist's waiting room, a restaurant that wanted the table back by eight, and a basement nobody liked. In 1976 the Society Library offered the members' room. We have been there since.

## What we do

Each meeting has a talk, a table of things to look at, and tea. Talks are given by members and run to about forty minutes; the rest of the evening is for questions, which here means arguments.

Through the year there is a summer lecture in a borrowed hall, an outing in August, a Christmas show and tell and a Christmas party, a stamp fair in February and a members' auction in the spring. In the winter we read aloud, usually from *Household Words*, the weekly magazine Charles Dickens edited in the 1850s, which was fascinated by the post. What we have read is in [Readings](/tagged/readings), and what we have done is in [Meetings](/tagged/meetings) and [Events](/tagged/events).

## How it is run

The club is run by [four officers](/tagged/people), elected every second March for two years, and by anyone else who stays late. The officers and the regulars are on the [Members](/members) page, and their addresses are on the [Contact](/contact) page. The club's [rules](/rules) fit on two sides of a card; the minutes of the annual meetings are under [Minutes](/tagged/minutes).

Dues are twelve dollars a year and pay for the room, the newsletter and very little else. Everything else, from the tea to the summer lecture hall, is paid for by the kettle fund, which is the hat that goes round at the end of each meeting. The first thing it bought, in 1951, was a kettle.

## A few habits

- We do not talk about the price of a stamp until after the tea.
- Tongs are provided. Fingers are discouraged.
- Visitors may ask any question, but the member who answers it will answer all of it.

If you would like to come along, [visit](/visit) on any second Tuesday, or [join](/join).
