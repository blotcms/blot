# Support the club

Dues pay for the room and the newsletter. Everything else is paid for by the kettle fund: a hat that goes round at the end of each meeting, and a line in the treasurer's book for anyone who would rather write a check. It is called the kettle fund because the first thing it bought, in 1951, was a kettle.

## What it buys

| Give | And it buys |
| --- | --- |
| $5 | A box of tea, which lasts two meetings |
| $12 | A year's dues for a junior member, who pays nothing |
| $25 | A pack of archival stock pages for the juniors' table |
| $60 | A new loupe, when the old one fails to come back |
| $150 | The deposit on Morrow Hall for the summer lecture |

Last year the fund raised a little over two thousand dollars, nearly all of it in the hat, and spent it on the things above, a new urn, and the hire of a quartet that came with the hall.

## How to give

Put something in the hat on a second Tuesday, or give it to Priya Venkataraman at the table by the door. She will write you a receipt. You can also send a check, made out to the New York Stamp Club, to the address on the [Contact](/contact) page.

> [!warning] Stamps are lovely, but they are not money
> Some members put stamps in the hat instead of cash, and we are always pleased to see them: they go to the juniors' table or the spring auction. But Priya can only give receipts for money, so a gift of stamps is not tax-deductible through the club. If that matters to you, give cash, and bring the stamps anyway.

Thank you. The tea is better than it was.
