# Visit

Meetings start at half past six on the second Tuesday of the month and end when the library closes, usually around nine. Visitors are welcome at any meeting, and nobody will ask you to join. Bring a stamp you can't identify.

## Where

A members' room at the Society Library on East Seventy-ninth Street, a short walk from the Lexington Avenue subway. Come in through the front door, say hello to the desk, and take the stairs on the left. The room is at the top, with the long table and the green lamps.

## Getting in

The reading room is up one flight of stairs. There is an elevator at the back of the building, past the desk; ask the staff and they will bring it down. The room has a step-free doorway, a good number of chairs with arms, and a table at a comfortable height for anyone using a wheelchair. If you need large-print programs or want to sit near the front, tell us in advance at the address on the [Join](/join) page and we will arrange it.

## What to bring

- A stamp, or an envelope, or a shoebox, that you would like identified.
- A magnifier, if you own one; we have a loupe, but it is in demand.
- A cookie, if you feel generous. Keep it away from the albums.

## Questions

<details><summary>Do I need to be a member to come?</summary>No. Anyone may attend a meeting as a guest, and most of our members started that way. We only ask that you sign the visitor's book.</details>

<details><summary>Is there a charge?</summary>No. Tea is free, and a hat goes round at the end for the kettle fund. Put in what you like.</details>

<details><summary>I know nothing about stamps. Is that a problem?</summary>It helps. The best questions come from people who have not read the catalog, and our members are happy to answer them at length.</details>

<details><summary>Can I bring my own collection?</summary>Please do. Put it on the long table before the talk and someone will find it. If you want it appraised for sale, we will tell you honestly what we think, and then send you to a dealer.</details>

<details><summary>Can children come?</summary>Yes, with a grown-up. Joyce Nakamura keeps a box of duplicates for first-time collectors under twelve.</details>

<details><summary>What if it rains?</summary>The meeting happens. The tea happens. The stamps stay in their sleeves, and the umbrellas go in the stand by the door.</details>
