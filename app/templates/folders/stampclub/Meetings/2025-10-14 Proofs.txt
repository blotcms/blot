# Proofs

A proof is a stamp before it is a stamp: the first pull from a finished plate, printed on card to show the engraver and the printer that the work is right. Ruth Abernethy brought four from the newspaper and periodical issue, and a small pot of tea in case anyone had to sit down.

The card proofs have a quality the stamps do not, because the ink sits crisply on stiff paper with no gum, no perforation and no postmark. The faces seem to look back at you. Ruth said she thinks this is the nearest she has come to an engraver's real intention, and that she is happy to be corrected.

An essay, by the way, is not an essay in the sense of an assignment. It is a trial design, submitted before the final one is chosen. The 3c Washington essay is among the oddest items in our shared album, because the face is right and everything around it is not. Leonard Frisch asked whether that made it an early draft. Ruth said, yes, exactly, and that is the point.
