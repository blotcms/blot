# Proofs

A proof is a stamp before it is a stamp: a pull from a finished plate, printed on card to show the engraver and the printer that the work is right. Ruth Abernethy brought images of three from the newspaper and periodical issue, and a small pot of tea in case anyone had to sit down.

The card proofs have a quality the stamps do not, because the ink sits crisply on stiff card with no gum, no perforation and no postmark. The faces seem to look back at you. Ruth said she thinks this is the nearest she has come to an engraver's real intention, and that she is happy to be corrected.

An essay, she explained, is not an essay in the sense of homework. It is a trial design, made before the final one is chosen. The 3c Washington essay was the oddest thing she showed, because the face is right and everything around it is not. Leonard Frisch asked whether that made it an early draft. Ruth said yes, exactly, and that is the point.
