# Grills

Priya Venkataraman keeps the club's accounts and, in her spare time, a drawer of grilled stamps. She says the two interests are related, because both are about stopping people from using the same thing twice.

A grill is a pattern of tiny pyramids pressed into the paper of a stamp, so that the cancelling ink soaks in and cannot be washed off.[^1] The Post Office introduced it in 1867 and dropped it within a few years, partly because the grill made the paper brittle and partly because nobody had ever washed a cancellation off a stamp on any scale worth worrying about.[^2]

She passed round a stamp under a magnifying glass, with the grill visible as a faint crosshatch. Her tip is to tilt it toward the light until the pattern appears. It can be seen from about a foot away, if you are patient and have good eyes.

[^1]: The grills were classified by size and shape, and the letter in each name (A, B, C, and so on) tells you which one you have.
[^2]: A grill is also a good way to tell a genuine stamp from a reprint, which is why Priya says every collector should own a loupe and a patient friend.
