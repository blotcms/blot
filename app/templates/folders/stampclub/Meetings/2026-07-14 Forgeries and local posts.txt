# Forgeries and local posts

Before the federal government had the city mail to itself, anyone with a satchel and an idea could carry it. Leonard Frisch, who set type for forty years, gave us the evening's longest talk and its most careful one.

## The private posts

{|<} Blood's was a Philadelphia post, founded in the 1840s, and one of the longest-lived. Allen's City Dispatch was a Chicago firm of the 1880s. The American Letter Mail Company carried letters between Boston, New York, Philadelphia and Baltimore from 1844, until a new postal law put it out of business.

From the 1840s on, private firms carried letters across American cities for a cent or two, under names such as City Dispatch and Despatch Post, and some ran between cities, undercutting the government's rates until the law caught up with them. Their stamps were simple, and many were printed from whatever type and plates the printer had to hand. Leonard says that is where the trouble starts: if a stamp is easy to print, it is easy to copy.

## How to look at one

{|<} Leonard recommends a 10x loupe, a good lamp, and a genuine example to compare against. The club's library has the standard reference on local posts; ask Constance.

Leonard's method is the typesetter's: do not look at the picture, look at the letters. A forger copies the design, but the spacing between the letters is the part nobody thinks to check. He showed us three tells:

- uneven gaps in the lettering
- a border that is slightly too regular
- ink that sits on the paper instead of in it

He also pointed out that some forgeries are now worth more than the stamps they copy, because they were made in small numbers and sold as curiosities in their own day. This led to a long argument that was not resolved, and a shorter one about supper.

![30c Eagle from the Great Central Sanitary Fair](/_stamps/30c Eagle Great Central Sanitary Fair single.jpg)

The last stamp he showed is not a forgery at all, but a charity stamp from the Civil War, sold at a fair in Philadelphia in 1864 to raise money for the Sanitary Commission. He included it, he said, because it is beautiful.
