# Sorted, stamped, numbered, filed

Constance Whitlock, our librarian, read *What We Do with Our Letters*, from *Household Words* in 1852. It follows a writer through a government office in London, where a clerk sorts, stamps, numbers, registers and files every letter that comes in. The full text is in [Readings](/tagged/readings).

The essay is a comic masterpiece of bureaucracy. Its hero will not open the small letters until the end, because he much prefers foolscap, and he keeps a press of folded letters beside a press of unfolded ones to prove his case. Constance read the printed pleas from the backs of government envelopes in the voice of a very tired woman, and the room did not recover for some time.

Members noted how many of the clerk's habits survive. Teddy Marchetti said the "received" stamp is still the thing a carrier looks for first. Our own treasurer said that the "date of the Board's minute" column is the one she would most like to add to the club ledger.
