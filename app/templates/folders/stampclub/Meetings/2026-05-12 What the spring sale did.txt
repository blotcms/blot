# What the spring sale did

Oscar Bellamy has run the club's spring auction since 2009 without once bidding at it, which he says is the auctioneer's only rule. Every May he reports on the March sale, and members rely on his report the way other people rely on the weather.

This year, he said, was a sale of two halves. The shoeboxes did well. The single stamps, which are what most of us bring, did not do much at all. This is, he reminded us, how it always goes. These are the club's own lots, consigned by members, with the club's own estimates:

| Lot | What it was | Estimate | Sold for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | $1 Lincoln Memorial, used, a little toned | $2 | $4 |
| 11 | $5 Head of Freedom, used, a crease through the dome | $10 | $8 |
| 27 | A shoebox of 1970s commemoratives, about two thousand, unsorted | $10 | $31 |
| 40 | A page of 1940s album copies of famous stamps, sold as copies | $1 | $3 |
| 52 | Teddy Marchetti's duplicate postage dues, on a stock page | $5 | $5 |

Oscar's rule for reading a table like this is to ignore the lots that went over and look at the ones that went under, because that is where people find out what they actually thought a stamp was worth. Lot 27 went to Marguerite Fong for the juniors' table, against spirited bidding from Inez Calloway, who wanted the box.

The pictures are fine copies, not the lots. The $24 Peace, a newspaper stamp that paid for bundles of newspapers by the pound, is the stamp Oscar would most like someone to consign one day. Nobody ever has. He has started to take it personally.
