# One head, three hundred million stamps

Ruth Abernethy, our president, read this month's essay: *The Queen's Head*, from *Household Words* in 1852, on how a single engraved head became nearly three hundred million stamps a year. The full text is in [Readings](/tagged/readings). She asked that nobody skip the section on gum, though the club already knew how it ended: last November Walter read us *The Great British Gum Secret*, which is the sequel, so we have now read the series backward.

The essay's great trick is arithmetic. One steel die, engraved over a fortnight, is hardened, pressed onto a soft steel roller, and the roller rolled onto plate after plate, 240 heads to a plate. By the authors' reckoning, an engraver doing it all by hand would still be at work had he started under Henry VIII. Ruth pointed out that the sums are very good, and that the Wandering Jew is a better employee than anyone we've met.

After the reading we did our own small version. Four members brought engraved heads from the American side of the family, and the loupe went round the room with the usual rule: you may look as long as you like, but you must say what you see. Walter Dziedzic spotted the fine lathework in the background of the Franklin before anyone else did. Ruth reminded us that the engine-turning was put there to defeat forgers, and that it has delighted collectors ever since.
