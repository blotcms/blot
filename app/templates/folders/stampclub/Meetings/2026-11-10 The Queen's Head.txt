# One head, three hundred million stamps

Ruth Abernethy, our president, read this month's essay: *The Queen's Head*, from *Household Words* in 1852, on how a single engraved head became some three hundred million stamps a year. The full text is in [Readings](/tagged/readings), and she asked that nobody skip the section on potato starch.

The authors' great trick is arithmetic. One steel die, engraved over a fortnight, is hardened into a "mother", pressed onto a soft roller, and the roller rolled onto plate after plate, 240 heads to a plate. By their reckoning, an engraver doing it all by hand would still be at work, having started under Henry VIII. Ruth pointed out that the sums are very good, and that the Wandering Jew is a better employee than anyone we've met.

After the reading we did our own small version. Four members brought engraved heads from the American side of the family, and a loupe went round the room with the usual rule: you may look as long as you like, but you must say what you see. Walter Dziedzic spotted the fine lathework in the background of the Franklin before anyone else did. Ruth reminded us that the Victorians put that engine-turning there to defeat forgers, and that it has delighted collectors ever since.
