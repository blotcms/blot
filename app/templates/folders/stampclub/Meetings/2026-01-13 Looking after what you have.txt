# Looking after what you have

The first meeting of the year was a practical one. Constance Whitlock, who looks after the Society Library's maps, gave a short and unsentimental talk on how stamps are lost. Mostly, she said, they are lost slowly, in the wrong cupboard.

Her rules fit on a card, and she handed out copies:

1. Keep stamps dry. A damp basement will do in a decade what a flood does in a night.
2. Keep them dark. Light fades the colors, and the reds go first, as the Victorians knew.
3. Keep them flat, in stock pages or archival mounts, and never in an album that closes with a snap.
4. Handle them with tongs, not fingers. Skin oils leave a mark you will not see for years.
5. Do not use tape. Ever. She said this several times.

She then turned to the hinge, the small folded rectangle of gummed paper our grandparents used to mount a stamp. A hinged stamp has been touched, and its gum is disturbed. A mint stamp with its original gum untouched is worth more, and Constance's view is that a stamp ought to be looked after for its own sake, not for its price.

Discussion turned to foxing, the brown freckles that appear on old paper, and several members brought examples. The room agreed they are best left alone and not discussed at dinner.
