# Columbus and the engravers

Walter Dziedzic carried mail in Queens for thirty-four years and says that every stamp he ever delivered looked like a Columbus. This turned out to be a joke. What he actually brought was a series of the 1869 pictorial issues, which were the first American stamps to show a scene instead of a portrait, and which were mocked for it.

The famous feature of these stamps is the frame, printed in one color and the center in another. The two had to be printed in separate passes, so the center could land slightly out of register. Walter showed us a set where the fleet sails a little low in its frame and said, *this is not an error, this is a mood*.

The two blue stamps, he said, are the classic confusion. One is a shade known as ultramarine, and the difference shows only in good daylight, so he held a window open for the room.

He closed with an essay we have not read at the club but recommend: *The Great British Gum Secret*, from 1852, which unmasks the poisonous gum of the penny stamp as potato starch. A fine thing, he said, to know before one licks anything.
