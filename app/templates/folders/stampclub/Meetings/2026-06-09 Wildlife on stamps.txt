# Wildlife on stamps

Joyce Nakamura teaches fourth grade and brought, in her words, "a class trip in an envelope". She had mounted eight stamps from the 1956 to 1971 wildlife conservation issues, each with a card on the back giving the animal's habitat, and she made us guess before she turned them over.

Most of the room did well with the turkey and the bison. Almost no one got the pronghorn, which Joyce says is the fastest land animal in North America and is not an antelope. Teddy Marchetti said that was typical of the Post Office. Joyce said that, on the contrary, the stamp is rather accurate.

She finished with the thing her pupils always ask, and read us the answer from the back of the last card:

> A stamp is a very small picture of a very large thing. The kindest thing to do is to look at it for as long as the thing deserves.

Joyce says this is not from a book. She wrote it on the bus. The room asked for a copy.
