# Wildlife on stamps

Marguerite Fong teaches fourth grade and brought, in her words, "a class trip in an envelope". She had mounted eight stamps from the wildlife conservation issues of 1956 to 1971, each with a card on the back giving the animal's habitat, and she made us guess before she turned them over.

Most of the room did well with the turkey and the bison. Almost no one got the pronghorn, which Marguerite says is the fastest land animal in North America and is not, whatever the stamp says, an antelope. Teddy Marchetti said that was typical of the Post Office. Marguerite said that in fairness, everyone called it an antelope in 1956.

Then the argument. Marguerite had brought three of the four butterflies of 1977 as a coda, and Inez Calloway asked whether a butterfly is wildlife or a flower that moves. The room divided. Inez held that a butterfly on a stamp is there to be pretty, like a flower, and belongs with the botanicals. Marguerite held that anything with six legs and a migration counts. Dr. Pruitt said that anything that flies is airmail, and was ruled out of order.

It was not resolved. Marguerite finished with the thing her pupils always ask, and read us the answer from the back of the last card:

> A stamp is a very small picture of a very large thing. The kindest thing to do is to look at it for as long as the thing deserves.

She says this is not from a book. She wrote it on the bus. The room asked for a copy.
