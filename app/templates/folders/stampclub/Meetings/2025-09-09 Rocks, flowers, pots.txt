# Rocks, flowers, pots

For thirty-five years Inez Calloway kept a stamp corner in her science classroom, and when she retired she took it home in a shoebox. She brought the shoebox to the club. The theme was natural things and things made by hand: four minerals, a set of wildflowers and a Pueblo pot, all issued between 1969 and 1977 and all, she says, better teaching aids than the textbook.

The Mineral Heritage set came first. Each shows a specimen against a dark ground, and Inez pointed out how well the printers caught the glassiness of the amethyst and the banding in the petrified wood.

Then the Botanical Congress set of 1969, with its Franklinia, a tree found in Georgia in 1765 and not seen in the wild since the early 1800s. Every living Franklinia, Inez said, comes from the seeds and cuttings one family of Philadelphia botanists took home, which is an argument for keeping seeds, and for keeping stamps.

The evening ended on the pot, and a good question from the back: did the designer see the pot, or a photograph of it? Nobody knew, and Inez wrote it on a card.
