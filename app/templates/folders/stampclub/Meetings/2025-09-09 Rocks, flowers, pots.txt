# Rocks, flowers, pots

Inez Calloway's science class has a stamp corner, and she brought it to the club in a shoebox. The theme was natural things made by hand: four minerals, a set of botanical stamps, and a Pueblo pot, all issued between 1969 and 1977 and all, she says, miniature masterpieces of printing.

The Mineral Heritage set came first. Each shows a specimen photographed against a dark background, and Inez pointed out how accurately the engravers caught the glassiness of the amethyst and the bandings in the petrified wood.

Then the botanical congress, with its famous Franklinia, a tree discovered in Georgia in 1765 and now extinct in the wild. Every living Franklinia, Inez said, is descended from seeds collected by one man, which is an argument for keeping seeds, and for keeping stamps.

![XIth International Botanical Congress, set of four](/_stamps/XIth International Botanical Congress, set of four.jpg)
![Botanical Congress, Franklinia](/_stamps/Botanical Congress Franklinia.jpg)

The evening ended on the pot, and a good question from the back: did the designer see the pot, or a photograph of it? Nobody knew, and Inez wrote it on a card.
