# Letters across the world

In 1974 the United States celebrated the Universal Postal Union with a set of stamps showing people writing and reading letters, taken from famous paintings. Constance Whitlock chose them as an August subject, partly because they are beautiful and partly because they are good company in a warm room.

The set is a small gallery. A woman reads, another writes, a man sits among old letters pinned to a board. Constance said the painters are Chardin, Terborch, Peto, Raphael, Hokusai, Liotard and Gainsborough, and that none of them ever thought about a postage stamp in their lives.

The second run of the evening was about the 1850 *Household Words* sketch *Valentine's Day at the Post-Office*, which Constance read out in parts: the sorters working through the night, the thousands of cards, and the clerk who looks up and says nothing. We recommend it for February.

Members were asked to write a letter before the next meeting and bring the envelope. Eleven did. One was returned undelivered, which pleased Teddy Marchetti more than anything else that evening.
