# A day at the Hartwell Mail Museum

In August we do not meet in the members' room, because the members' room is hot. Instead Walter Dziedzic, who plans the outing every year, took us on a day trip to the Hartwell Mail Museum in Poughkeepsie, a small, cheerful place in a former depot, run by volunteers who clearly know more about mail than most of the people who deliver it.

Fourteen of us took the 9:12 north, and the best seat on the train, by common consent, was the one beside Walter while he explained how mail used to be snatched from a moving train by a hook and a crane at the side of the track. Nobody believed this until the museum showed us the crane.

The collection includes a wooden sorting frame with ninety-six pigeonholes, a pair of canvas bags still labeled in stencil, and a case of early stamps and carriers' labels. The volunteers let us handle a few under supervision. We spent a long time at the case of stamps from the private city posts of the 1840s and 1850s, and Leonard Frisch identified the typeface on one of the labels before the guide had finished the sentence.

On the way home Constance read us *Post to Australia*, from *Household Words* in 1856, in which a merchant's clerk, tired of eighty days at sea each way, proposes boring a tube through the earth to Melbourne.
