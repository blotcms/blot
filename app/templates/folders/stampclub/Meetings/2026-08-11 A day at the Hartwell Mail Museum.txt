# A day at the Hartwell Mail Museum

In August we do not meet in the reading room, because the reading room is hot. Instead Inez Calloway organized a day trip to the Hartwell Mail Museum in Poughkeepsie, a small, cheerful institution in a former depot, run by volunteers who clearly know more about mail than the Post Office.

Eleven of us took the 9:12 north, and the best seat on the train, by common consent, was the one where Walter Dziedzic explained how mail used to be hooked from a moving train by a crane at the side of the track. Nobody believed this until the museum showed us the crane.

The collection includes a wooden sorting frame with ninety-six pigeonholes, a pair of canvas bags still labelled in stencil, and a case of early stamps and carrier labels. The volunteers let us handle a few under supervision. We spent a long time at the case of stamps printed in the 1860s for privately run city posts, and Leonard Frisch, our typesetter, identified the typeface on one of the labels before the guide had finished the sentence.

We read a short piece on the way home, *Post to Australia*, from *Household Words* in 1856, which describes a letter that took three months. Everyone agreed this was a good deal faster than our own treasurer's last reply.
