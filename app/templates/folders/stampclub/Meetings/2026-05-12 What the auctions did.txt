# What the auctions did

Oscar Bellamy has attended every major stamp auction in the city for twenty years without, as he puts it, having bid. He reports instead, and members rely on his reports the way other people rely on the weather.

This spring, he said, it was a season of two halves. The great rarities did well. The ordinary stamps, which are the ones most of us own, did not do much at all. This is, he reminded us, how it always goes.

| Lot | Estimate | Realized | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $1 Lincoln Memorial, mint | $90 | $140 | A clean example |
| $5 Head of Freedom, mint | $200 | $190 | Slight crease |
| $24 Peace, newspaper issue | $1,800 | $2,600 | Pressing demand |
| 90c Lincoln, G grill | $450 | $410 | A little off-center |

Oscar's rule for reading a table like this is to ignore the ones that went over and look at the ones that went under, because that is where people find out what they actually thought a stamp was worth. He added, for the benefit of newer members, that auction estimates are made by people who want you to come to the auction.
