# The Post-Mistress and a Christmas show

December is our lightest meeting. We read, we look at things, and we eat. Teddy Marchetti chose the reading this year: *The Post-Mistress*, from *Household Words* in 1855, in which a governess in Wensleydale buys a shilling's worth of stamps from an old woman who has run the village post office through four reigns, and is still owed the rest of them eighteen months later. Teddy said that this is exactly how his own mother ran things in the Bronx, minus the ghosts. The full text is in [Readings](/tagged/readings).

Afterwards the members laid out their Christmas stamps on the long table. The field is smaller than you would think, because the first American Christmas stamp appeared only in 1962, and members have strong feelings about all of them since. The favorite, by show of hands, was the Currier and Ives winter scene, which two members said they had sent to the same aunt for twelve years.
