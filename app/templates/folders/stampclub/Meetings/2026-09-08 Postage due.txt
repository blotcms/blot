# Postage due

A short evening, by request, because the library was setting up for a lecture on ferns.

Teddy Marchetti carried mail in Brooklyn for forty years and spoke about the stamp he most disliked delivering. A postage due stamp is the mark of a letter that did not carry enough postage, and it was the recipient who paid. Teddy's view is that a carrier learns early to read a doorstep: some people hand over the extra two cents with a smile, and some people say they will write to the Postmaster General. He had both on his route, sometimes on the same floor.

He passed round three used examples and explained how clerks tallied the amounts due. We thought it was a fine fit for a club that has, on occasion, also been caught short.
