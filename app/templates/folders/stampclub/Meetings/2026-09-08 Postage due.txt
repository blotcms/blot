# Postage due

A short evening, by request, because the library was setting up for a lecture on ferns.

Teddy Marchetti carried mail in Brooklyn for forty years and spoke about the stamp he least liked delivering. A postage due stamp is the mark of a letter that did not carry enough postage, and it was the person receiving it who paid. Teddy's view is that a carrier learns early to read a doorstep: some people hand over the extra two cents with a smile, and some say they will write to the Postmaster General. He had both on his route, sometimes on the same floor.

He passed round three used examples and explained how clerks tallied the amounts due. On his last day on the route, in 1998, a woman on Union Street paid him nine cents she said she had owed the Post Office since the Carter administration, and asked for a receipt. We thought it a fine subject for a club that has, on occasion, also been caught short.
