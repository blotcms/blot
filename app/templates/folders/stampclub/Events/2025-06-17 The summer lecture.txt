# The summer lecture

Once a year the club borrows a proper hall, puts up a notice in the library and invites anyone who wants to come. This year three hundred people did, which is roughly two hundred and forty more than usually hear us talk about postmarks.

![The hall from the back, the audience in the dark facing a lit stage](/_events/auditorium-from-the-back.jpg)
![A wide, dark auditorium with four musicians in a pool of light](/_events/auditorium-quartet-wide.jpg)
![Two chairs on a pale paneled stage, a conversation under way](/_events/auditorium-two-chairs.jpg)

Ruth Abernethy spoke for forty minutes on the post road: the riders, the inns where the bags changed hands, and the rates, which in 1792 made a single sheet sent from Boston to New York cost a good part of a day's wage. She took questions for another twenty, the best of which was whether anyone had ever been fined for reading someone else's mail. (Yes.)

Afterwards a string quartet played, because the hall came with one and it seemed rude not to. Several members stayed until the ushers turned the lights up, then stayed a little longer.
