# The Valentine's tango

Last summer Constance Whitlock read us *Valentine's Day at the Post-Office*, the 1850 piece in *Household Words* in which two men post three letters in Fleet Street on the fourteenth of February and follow them through the sorting rooms at St. Martin's-le-Grand. The authors end by insisting that their letters were not valentines. Constance said that was the saddest sentence in the magazine, and that the club owed the Post Office a dance. This year it paid up.

![Couples dancing tango on a mosaic floor under marble columns](/_events/great-hall-tango.jpg)

A teacher from a dance school on Amsterdam Avenue gave us an hour's lesson and then left us to it. Nobody fell over. Teddy Marchetti, who carried letters for forty years, said it was the first time he had been on a marble floor without a bag on his shoulder.
